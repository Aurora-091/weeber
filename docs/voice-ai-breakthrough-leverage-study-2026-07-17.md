# Voice AI breakthrough & leverage study — 2026

**Date:** 2026-07-17. Triggered by: "why won't people switch to us when they're already using Bolna/Retell/Bland, and what can we actually pick up from studying the technology itself — speech/voice models, STT, TTS, OpenAI's new voice stuff, HuggingFace, what's out there but underused." Grounded in real sources pulled this session (OpenAI's own May 2026 announcement, ElevenLabs' own architecture writeup, Kyutai's model pages, live pricing) — not assumed.

## Part 1 — why existing users don't just leave, and what that means for Weeber

Real research on this (voice AI vendor-lock-in threads, LLM migration cost writeups) converges on one point: **the lock-in was never the API or the phone numbers — it's the sunk cost in prompt/pathway engineering.** A team that spent weeks tuning a Retell conversation flow, testing edge cases, and getting call-success rates to an acceptable number isn't going to throw that away for a marginally better STT accuracy number. Numbers can be ported; institutional tuning effort can't. That's the actual moat every incumbent in this market has, Weeber included eventually.

**What that means concretely:**
- Weeber will never win existing Bolna/Retell/Bland customers by being "also capable of the same thing." Feature parity doesn't overcome switching cost.
- Weeber wins on **new** buyers who haven't chosen yet (the actual target — SMBs that haven't adopted any voice AI), and on buyers whose incumbent platform has hit a wall the incumbent structurally can't fix (see Part 3 — the architecture ceiling).
- The one lever that *does* overcome sunk-cost inertia: something the incumbent's architecture genuinely can't do, not just does slightly worse. That's where the technical research below matters — not "our TTS sounds nicer," but "we solved a problem their architecture can't solve without a rebuild."

**Weeber's actual leverage right now, stated plainly:** every competitor scanned in the changelog review (`docs/competitor-changelog-scan-2026-07-17.md`) is running a **cascaded architecture with years of production traffic already committed to it.** Re-platforming that — even partially — means re-certifying reliability against real customer SLAs, mid-flight. Weeber has ~10 real calls total. That's not a weakness to apologize for, it's the one window in this business's whole lifecycle where the *right* architecture costs the same to build as the *familiar* one. That window closes the moment there's real paying volume depending on today's choices.

## Part 2 — the actual architecture spectrum (not a binary)

ElevenLabs' own engineering writeup (`elevenlabs.io/blog/cascaded-vs-fused-models`, published Mar 2026, updated Jul 2026 — i.e. current) lays out five real architectures, not two:

| # | Architecture | What it is | Where Weeber is today | Where the trust/reasoning ceiling is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Basic Cascaded** | Separate STT → LLM → TTS, plain text handoff | **This is Weeber, exactly.** | Sounds "functional but flat" — no prosody/tone control, this is the literal gap the "sounds robotic" complaints trace back to. |
| 2 | **Advanced Cascaded** | Same modular chain, but the LLM emits *delivery* instructions ("reassuringly," "with urgency") alongside the words, co-located stack (no network hops), richer turn-taking signals | **Not yet built.** | None of the trust/control loses — this is the free upgrade. |
| 3 | **Hybrid** | Acoustic features (pitch, emotion) fed as embeddings straight into the LLM, TTS stays modular | Not applicable to Weeber's compliance needs yet | LLM becomes hard to audit/swap |
| 4 | **Sequential Fused** | One multimodal model does STT+LLM+TTS in a single pass (OpenAI's GPT-Realtime family, one call at a time) | Not used | Weak guardrails without a text layer; lighter reasoning cores historically — **this gap is now closing fast, see Part 3** |
| 5 | **Duplex Fused** | Simultaneous listen+speak, most natural short exchanges, least controllable (Kyutai Moshi lives here) | Not used | Hardest to guardrail/debug/log — genuinely unsuitable for a compliance-first product as the *primary* path today |

**The actionable read for Weeber: #2 (Advanced Cascaded) is the free, zero-risk upgrade sitting right there.** It's exactly what Retell's *Colloquial Model* and *Expressive Mode* (shipped June, per the changelog scan) and ElevenLabs' own *Expressive Mode* already are. Concretely, for this codebase: the LLM's `onTextDelta` stream currently only carries words — extending it to also carry a delivery tag (calm / empathetic / urgent / upbeat) that maps to whatever prosody control the active TTS provider exposes (Cartesia and ElevenLabs both support emotion/delivery parameters already, per this session's own provider files) is a real, scoped, buildable feature — not a research project.

## Part 3 — what's actually new (and where the ceiling on #4/#5 is moving)

**OpenAI's May 7, 2026 announcement** ("Advancing voice intelligence with new models in the API") shipped three models that matter here, and the framing itself signals where the field is racing:

- **GPT-Realtime-2** — first realtime (sequential fused) model with GPT-5-class reasoning. Adjustable reasoning effort (minimal → xhigh), preambles ("let me check that" while a tool call runs — Weeber already built this exact pattern independently as `maybePlayToolCallFiller`, good validation the instinct was right), parallel tool calls with audible transparency, and — the actual headline — **Zillow reports a 26-point call-success lift on their hardest adversarial benchmark (95% vs 69%) and specifically calls out Fair Housing compliance robustness** as production-viable. That's a fused model doing real regulated-industry guardrail work, which directly narrows the "fused = can't be trusted for compliance" gap ElevenLabs' own article leans on. Worth revisiting this comparison again in 6 months, not treating it as settled.
- **GPT-Realtime-Translate** — 70+ input languages, 13 output, built for live translation while keeping pace with natural speech. **Bolna's own CTO is quoted directly in OpenAI's announcement**: *"Building voice AI for India means handling diverse regional phonetics. In our evals across Hindi, Tamil, and Telugu, GPT-Realtime-Translate delivered 12.5% lower Word Error Rates than any other model we tested."* This is real competitive intelligence, not speculation — Bolna is already evaluating this specific model for the exact Hindi/Tamil/Telugu problem Weeber solved differently this month (ElevenLabs Scribe + pronunciation dictionary). Worth a real side-by-side test before assuming the current approach is the ceiling.
- **GPT-Realtime-Whisper** — streaming STT, priced at $0.017/min, competitive with Deepgram/Sarvam's own per-minute STT pricing already in Weeber's cost tiers.

**Cost reality check, not just capability:** GPT-Realtime-2 at low reasoning effort (the default) runs roughly **$1.15/hour of conversation** ($32/$64 per 1M audio input/output tokens); at xhigh reasoning (the tier the impressive benchmark numbers are measured at) it's closer to **$4.61/hour**. Weeber's own documented all-in cascaded COGS target is ~$0.06/min (~$3.60/hour). So a fused GPT-Realtime-2 agent at low/default reasoning could genuinely be *cheaper* than today's cascaded stack, not more expensive — the assumption that fused models cost more doesn't hold at the low-reasoning tier. It only gets expensive at the reasoning levels needed to match frontier-LLM-class tool-calling reliability, which is exactly where the real tradeoff actually lives (cost vs benchmark-grade reasoning), not "fused is always pricier."

## Part 4 — the open-source frontier (real leverage, because self-hostable)

- **Kyutai Moshi** (`kyutai-labs/moshi`, open weights on HuggingFace) — a genuine duplex-fused model, 7B, dual-stream architecture (an inner text "monologue" stream generated in parallel with the audio stream — a clever partial answer to the "fused models lose the inspectable text layer" problem, since Moshi's inner monologue *is* an inspectable text trace, just not the same thing as a cascaded pipeline's clean STT output). Real, cited latency: **160ms theoretical, ~200ms in practice** — categorically faster than any cascaded pipeline can get (cascaded pipelines: 1.5-3s typical in production per multiple 2026 sources, matches exactly what this session measured on Weeber's own calls: ~1.7-2.1s). Open weights means this is genuinely self-hostable — no per-minute vendor bill, no dependency on OpenAI/ElevenLabs pricing changes.
- **Kyutai Pocket TTS** (Jan 2026, `kyutai-labs/pocket-tts`) — 100M parameters (~16x smaller than typical production TTS models), runs real-time **on CPU**, no GPU required, with voice cloning support. This is the single most disruptive line item in this whole study for Weeber specifically: if it holds up in testing, it means TTS could move from "per-minute vendor API call over the network" to "runs on the same box as the rest of the call handler" — eliminating both the cost-per-minute *and* the network round-trip that's currently part of every `ttsFirstByteMs` measurement this session already instrumented. Worth a real prototype, not just a read.
- **HuggingFace's own `speech-to-speech` reference repo** exists specifically to make this kind of self-hosted pipeline buildable without starting from a research paper — a genuine on-ramp, not a moonshot.

## Part 5 — concrete, tiered recommendation

**Tier 1 — build now, zero architectural risk (Advanced Cascaded upgrade):** extend the LLM's turn output to carry a delivery/tone tag alongside text, map it to the active TTS provider's existing emotion/delivery controls (Cartesia and ElevenLabs both already expose this). This is the same move Retell and ElevenLabs both just shipped as flagship features. Directly answers the "sounds robotic" quality bar without touching compliance, guardrails, or the failover/latency work already shipped this session.

**Tier 2 — prototype now, decide with real data (GPT-Realtime-Translate for Hindi/Hinglish):** given a *direct competitor* is publicly quoted getting a measurably better WER on exactly Weeber's hardest language problem, this deserves a real side-by-side test call against the current ElevenLabs Scribe + pronunciation-dictionary setup before assuming the current approach is as good as it gets. Cheap to test ($0.034/min), doesn't require abandoning anything already built.

**Tier 3 — real prototype, not production, this quarter (Kyutai Pocket TTS self-host):** worth an actual spike — stand up Pocket TTS on a Railway service, run it through the same `ConnectTts` interface Weeber's provider registry already uses (it's designed for exactly this — adding a provider is mechanical, per this session's own `llm/index.ts` pattern discussion), and measure real TTFB/quality against Cartesia. If it holds up, this is a structural cost and latency advantage no cascaded-architecture incumbent can match without also self-hosting — and Weeber is small enough right now to actually do that operational work; Bolna/Retell/Bland at their current scale are not going to re-architect their entire TTS layer for a 100M-parameter model, however good it is.

**Tier 4 — do not build yet (full duplex-fused as the primary path):** Moshi-class duplex models are the most exciting technology in this whole study and the least appropriate *default* architecture for a product whose entire wedge is "compliance-first, TCPA/DNC baked in, disclosure must be provably spoken." The lack of an inspectable, guardrail-enforceable text layer is a real, current limitation for that specific use case — not FUD, confirmed independently by both ElevenLabs' own engineering writeup and OpenAI's own framing of GPT-Realtime-2 as the *bridge* toward reasoning-capable fused models, not evidence duplex models are already there. Worth keeping the architecture modular enough to trial on one narrow, non-compliance-critical flow (an FAQ bot, not a cart-recovery or COD-confirmation call) once it's actually been tested, not assumed.

## The honest answer to "why won't they just switch to us"

They won't, for the reasons in Part 1 — and chasing them with feature parity is the wrong game entirely. The real opportunity is the *next* SMB that hasn't picked a platform yet, plus the structural bet that a pre-scale product can adopt the right architecture (Tier 1 today, Tier 2/3 proven out before committing) while every incumbent in the changelog scan is stuck re-optimizing a cascaded pipeline they already have millions of production minutes riding on. That's the leverage — not better, just able to move before it's expensive to.
